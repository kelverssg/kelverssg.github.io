Financial Performance

Company Narratives — Transcript Level

Aon (AON) — Execution Engine  CEO Greg Case · CFO Edmund Reese

  • Commercial Risk +7% organic — 4th consecutive quarter at 6%+. New business contributed 9 points. Drivers: construction (double-digit), data centres (pipeline 3× vs last year), M&A services, North America, EMEA.
  • Reinsurance Solutions +4% organic — growth in treaty placements despite soft property-cat market (press release: "organic revenue growth of 4% reflects growth in treaty placements").
  • AI infrastructure: Broker Copilot and Claims Copilot in production. Broker Copilot delivers real-time pricing and trading analytics at point of sale; Claims Copilot consolidates data across geographies and lines.
  • $1.3B cumulative investment in talent and technology by year-end — framed as embedded producer infrastructure, not cost-out.
  • FCF $363M (+332% YoY) — working capital improvement; full-year FCF growth reaffirmed.
  • No major M&A; capital returned via buybacks; cleanest Q1 narrative / no major integration or litigation overhang.
Structural read: Aon's 39.1% margin is the widest in the sector and widening. Commercial Risk's 4-quarter consistency at ≥6% organic is the strongest execution signal in the peer group. The $1.3B AI investment is a producer productivity story — which compounds margin while sustaining revenue growth.

WTW (WTW) — Transition Risk  CEO Carl Hess · CFO Andrew Krasner · Chief AI Officer Spike Lipkin

  • R&B: 2% organic (or 1% ex book-of-business) — Lucy Clarke: "Q1 was a softer growth quarter for CRB, reflecting an exceptionally strong prior year comparable."
  • New business miss: "The majority of the below-plan results is due to a miss in our new business target, some of which will time into future quarters." April normalised.
  • HWC outperformed: Health +6%, Wealth +4%, Career –3% (Middle East geopolitical disruption).
  • Margin nuance: Overall company adj op margin +70bps to 22.3% (press release). R&B segment specifically +60bps to 22.6% (transcript); R&B underlying margin +10bps only (ex-FX, book-of-business, acquisitions — Krasner, earnings call). The market read the underlying — hence –12% stock reaction.
  • Newfront integration: Coverage Gap Analysis, Navigator, Partner Management deployed. Early multistate healthcare provider win.
  • Spike Lipkin (Chief AI Officer): "Colleagues who use our technology sell about 50% more than those who do not. Client attrition drops by half when clients use our tools."
  • Carl Hess: "AI can inform decisions, but it does not negotiate with carriers, advocate on behalf of insurers."
  • Competitive pricing: "continued deterioration... particularly in the large and complex segment" — WTW's core market.
Guidance: R&B mid-single-digit (narrowed). Target: 100bps avg margin expansion per year over next 2 years. Newfront integration is the execution dependency.
Structural read: Underlying +10bps margin is the tell — the rest is FX and acquisition noise. WTW is in a cost-vs-invest balancing act. Newfront adds distribution capacity but integration risk is real. The –12% is the market pricing execution optionality, not penalising a bad business.

Marsh McLennan (MRSH) — Scale + Consulting Buffer  CEO John Doyle · CFO Mark McGivney

  • Revenue $7.60B (+8% reported, +4% underlying) — beat $7.38B estimate. Adj op margin 31.8% — flat YoY (0bps; 31.8% in both Q1 2026 and Q1 2025 per reconciliation table). GAAP margin fell sharply due to $425M Greensill charge.
  • Guy Carpenter weak spot: +2% underlying — John Doyle: "It's not likely to be Guy Carpenter's best growth year this year." Record 7 cat bond issuances; $2B new third-party capital; non-loss account rates –15–20%.
  • Marsh Agency: Double-digit new business growth; transaction risk and construction double-digit.
  • Asia Pacific +5% underlying — outperforming the –5% regional pricing environment; consistent with net new client acquisition and exposure growth.
  • Oliver Wyman: AI Quotient team "fastest-growing practice" — advised on >$50B AI capital deployment in Q1.
  • Mercer: $1.7B (+11%, +5% underlying); AUM $727B (+19% YoY).
  • AI tools in production: ADA, Centrus, Marsh Risk Cortex, Claims IQ (~$200B loss data, thousands of colleagues), GC Quotebox, Blue[i]. Document ingestion: "thousands weekly, improving efficiency by 20%."
  • Greensill: $425M charge — "best estimate of liability... influenced by court-sponsored mediation." Excluded from adjusted figures; liability updated after mediation, matter remains unresolved.
Structural read: Consulting (~34% of revenue) is the structural counter-cyclical buffer. Marsh's ability to grow 4% underlying while the GIMI composite falls –5% globally is the clearest proof that relationship depth and consulting ballast insulate a P&C broker from the pricing cycle better than pure risk placement scale.

Insurance Pricing Environment (Marsh GIMI, Q1 2026)

7th consecutive quarter of overall price declines. Composite: –5% globally. US Casualty is the sole major hardening pocket (+9%); US Excess Casualty +18% (social inflation / nuclear verdicts). Property, cyber, and D&O continue softening.

Asia / Pacific Deep-Dive

Asia / Pacific Pricing — Key Findings

  • ASIA Overall: –5% (in line with global average)
  • ASIA Property: –5% — moderating faster off prior cycle peaks vs global –9%
  • PACIFIC Overall: –12% — most aggressive softening of any region
  • PACIFIC Property: –14% — steepest decline globally after AU/NZ catastrophe re-rating
  • JAPAN Property Cat (Apr 1): –15–20% on risk-adjusted basis
  • IMEA –10%; UK –8%; Europe –5%
Strategic read: Pacific property at –14% (Japan –15–20% April 1) signals post-2022 catastrophe re-rating fully running through. This is a structural buyers' market for regional corporates. Marsh Asia Pacific's +5% underlying growth in this environment is consistent with net new client acquisition and exposure growth — not pricing pass-through. WTW has no comparable Asia Pacific data point.

Synthesis — Cross-Competitor Dynamics

1. Execution Leaders vs Transition Risk

Aon and Marsh both executing cleanly in a soft-pricing environment — Commercial Risk +7% and Marsh +4% underlying both beat expectations. WTW's +3% organic (or +2% ex-R&B book-of-business) and underlying +10bps margin is the laggard. WTW remains a transition case pending Newfront landing.

2. The Casualty Tailwind — Who Benefits Most

US Casualty (+9%) and US Excess Casualty (+18%) are the sole pricing tailwinds globally. Aon's Commercial Risk mix skews toward large/complex US casualty — structural near-term tailwind as social inflation and nuclear verdicts sustain hardening while property corrects. Marsh benefits but is partially offset by Guy Carpenter's soft property-cat exposure.

3. AI — Infrastructure vs Marketing

Aon ($1.3B): embedded productivity at point of sale (Broker Copilot). Marsh: operational efficiency + advisory leverage (Claims IQ, Oliver Wyman AI Quotient, GC Quotebox). WTW's Spike Lipkin 50% sales uplift stat is the most commercially specific claim across the three calls — if durable, it's the strongest AI productivity argument in the sector.

4. Asia Pacific — Structural Opportunity in Soft Markets

Soft markets favour incumbents with captive distribution and large-account relationships — Marsh and Aon over WTW in this region. Pacific at –12% compresses margin per placement but creates volume expansion opportunity as insured values grow. Marsh +5% confirms this dynamic. WTW's absence of regional data is itself a signal.

5. Guidance Confidence Hierarchy

Aon: Full-year reaffirmed — highest conviction. FCF +332% supports the story.  |  Marsh: Mid-single-digit maintained — credible; consulting provides downside buffer.  |  WTW: R&B guidance narrowed — lower conviction; Newfront integration dependency. Market priced in the risk (–12%).
